Route reality

Yes, the bike can work. But only if we choose the right atoll.

Most postcard resort islands are too small for meaningful cycling. Addu is different: Gan, Feydhoo, Maradhoo, Maradhoo-Feydhoo and Hithadhoo are connected by road, creating a real endurance corridor while the surrounding lagoon supplies the water stages.

Illustrative race geometry only. Exact swim lines, transitions, road closures and protected-area access require local survey, permits, safety boats and traffic management.
Connected road, not a resort loop

Visit Maldives describes a 16 km link road connecting the western Addu islands from Hithadhoo to Gan. That is enough to build controlled repeats instead of riding circles around a tiny private island.

Official source ↗
A protected finish worth earning

Addu Nature Park combines wetlands, mangroves and cycling routes. Race access would need to respect protected zones, so the final approach is a planning concept rather than a promised course.

Protected-area source ↗
Gan makes the logistics credible

Gan International Airport sits at the southern end of the corridor. Visit Maldives reports roughly 1.5 hours by air from Velana International Airport, which keeps bikes, staff and guests inside a manageable transfer chain.

Travel source ↗
Bike handling is a product feature

Guests do not drag bike boxes across jetties. The event sells white-glove mechanics, secured transport, pre-stage setup and post-race packing as part of the experience.
