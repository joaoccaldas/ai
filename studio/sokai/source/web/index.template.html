Craft history · museum view

From steel to mounted sword.

A non-procedural overview of traditional Japanese swordmaking. Methods varied by period, school and smith.

01

Material

Sword steel is associated with tatara-smelted material, including tamahagane, selected by the smith for its qualities.

02

Refinement

Repeated heating, forge-welding and folding consolidated the steel and managed its carbon. Practice was not universal.

03

Construction

Blades could combine steels in different internal constructions. The tang (nakago) is integral to the blade.

04

Hardening

A clay coating before quenching let edge and body harden differently. The hamon is a trace of that transformation, not paint.

05

Polishing

Specialist polishers reveal the geometry, the hada grain and the activity of the hamon.

06

Mountings

Other masters made the koshirae: guard, fittings, lacquered saya, ray-skin grip and silk wrap — works of art in themselves.

Provenance · how it was made

A contemporary study.

Blender master. The katana, stand, atelier, garden and vault were modeled procedurally in Blender 5.2: real blade cross-sections along an 18 mm sori, woven ito ribbons crossing over and under, Boolean-cut sukashi in the tsuba. Lighting was path-traced in Cycles and baked into 4K lightmaps; the reflections on the steel come from HDR panoramas rendered in Cycles from the sword’s own position.

Realtime. Three.js r186, WebGL 2, physically based materials with clearcoat, sheen, anisotropy and iridescence; wet-floor reflections, bloom, depth of field and a film grade. Hamon, lacquer, silk and metal maps are generated in your browser, so every choice in the Forge is live.

Infinite. The theme — lives that remember, and an object that carries memory across them — is an original homage inspired by the film Infinite (2021). No footage, logos, characters or designs from the film are used.

An artistic reconstruction, not a scan, historical certification or fabrication guide. No accounts, analytics, remote fonts or network requests. Engine: Three.js (MIT).
