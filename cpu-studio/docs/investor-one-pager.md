# CPU Studio: Build Through Time | Funding Memo | 23 October 2026

**Stage:** playable, dependency-free vertical slice. **Historical investor draft as of pre-GitHub deployment; see release-gate.md for current publishing status.** No publicly verified deployment or real-user traction as of this document. **Company context:** candidate educational gaming product for onda.ai (organizational/repository ownership not yet established in GitHub).

## 30-second pitch

Young people use computers every day but often don't understand the machines inside. CPU Studio lets them build, repair and collect computers across 50 years of technology. Players start with a 1975 switch-and-LED kit and progressively discover the 1981 PC, multimedia desktops, LAN-era gaming systems and 2026 creator workstations. Every failure tells them *why*, and every successful build unlocks a new era.

## Product and differentiation

- **Simple on mobile:** no login, no setup, tap hardware parts into visible slots, POWER button always reachable.
- **Chronological progression:** **10 levels across 5 eras**; the room, hardware, color, display and visual language evolve in-step with the technology, but navigation stays familiar.
- **Evidence-backed education:** real interface distinctions such as 256 bytes vs 1 KB, 5¼-inch floppies, EDO SIMMs, IDE, DDR vs DDR2, DDR5/NVMe/AM5. Representative correct configurations; not a physical hardware emulator.
- **Collecting and credits:** shop with 27 historically relevant components, credits earned through actual learning builds, permanent museum achievements, collectible artifacts, and deterministic NPC barter.
- **Social wedge:** safe read-only blueprint links and friend-to-friend explanation. No public chat or unverified trading in this version.

## Why now, not why we're guaranteed to win

PC Building Simulator demonstrates appeal for hardware assembly. NandGame and Turing Complete validate deep computing education. Roblox's game-design literature recommends instant onboarding, clear core loops, progressive goals and social motivation. CPU Studio combines historical immersion with highly constrained, cause-and-effect learning. **This positioning is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated market gap or investor return.**

## Product actually delivered

Static-source web game; no npm runtime dependencies, external APIs or hosted state. Deterministic engine with **29 passing Node tests**, including end-to-end state-machine progression and DOM adapter smoke tests. Browser navigation was not verified in the development environment due to managed Chromium policy and remains a beta blocker before calling this publicly launch-ready.

## Monetization tests, not assumptions

1. Free educational core plus optional caregiver/educator 'challenge labs' (only after learning validation).
2. B2B classroom licenses for teacher missions and progress reports, if schools show procurement demand and suitable privacy terms.
3. Licensed museum/education partnerships with hardware-history institutions after rights clearance.

No child-directed targeted ads, microtransaction design, paid random rewards or real-money player trading in the proposal.

## Pilot and funding decision gates

- 20–30 guardian-consented first-time players, diverse age and prior knowledge.
- Hypothesized target: 80% first successful boot within two minutes without coaching.
- Hypothesized target: at least 65% successfully diagnose an unseen hardware mismatch.
- Measurable pre/post comprehension improvement, ideally compared against a static lesson.
- At least 5 actionable parent/teacher distribution interviews, with verified willingness to use/pay separated from generic interest.
- Independent historical reviewer, child safety/privacy review and cross-device browser QA before external beta.

**Capital ask:** Not responsibly specified until pilot evidence, resource requirements, commercial model and legal obligations are measured. Funding request should buy validated learning outcomes, curriculum/content depth, user acquisition tests, and safe infrastructure, not a speculative multiplayer marketplace.

## Risks and mitigations

- **Novelty/retention:** 10 levels are finite. Test replayable genuine engineering challenges rather than addictive economies.
- **Accuracy:** authored machine constraints must undergo period-specific expert review and transparent simplification notes.
- **Multiplayer safety:** real trading is expensive, fraud-prone and especially risky with minors. Keep local NPC demo now; design audited server-backed escrow only if user research supports it.
- **Distribution:** schools have slower purchasing; test parent and after-school channels too.
- **Product maturity:** no actual cohort statistics, QA matrix, database scalability evidence or audit yet.
