# CPU Studio / product specification / v0.2

## One sentence

Build and troubleshoot personal computers across five technology eras, while the player's workshop, interface and skills evolve with computing history.

## Motivation and unique advantage

Most computer games emphasize either realistic hardware assembly (high cognitive load) or gate-and-logic puzzles (abstract). CPU Studio uses **historically accurate progression + instant causality + collection loops** to bridge both worlds. The testable hypothesis is that historical, interactive cause-effect repairs improve understanding and bring players back voluntarily.

## Primary learner and jobs to be done

- **Learner 9–14** (hypothesis): 'I want to build a working machine, understand why it runs, and show friends my creation.'
- **Caregiver/educator** (hypothesis): 'I want a safe, approachable way to introduce computing hardware and evaluate whether a child learned something.'
- **Older nostalgic learner** (secondary hypothesis): 'I want to explore the evolution of PC technology and build era-specific projects.'

## Navigation and onboarding

At all times there are only **4 bottom tabs**: Build / Shop / Museum / Club. The first session starts on Build. No registration, onboarding wizard, forced tutorial, quizzes before play or payment wall. 1975 introductory kit contains exactly three parts. Interaction pattern remains invariant across eras: tap part → visible slot → power → diagnosis/reward. Complexity is introduced in the content, not in repeatedly redesigning controls.

## World chronology and mechanics

| Levels | World / visual changes | Technology | Skills learned |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | **1975 / warm garage** · black kit, toggles, amber LEDs | 8080-class kit, S-100, 256B → 1KB | CPU, memory, manual input, expansion |
| 3–4 | **1981 / bedroom desk** · beige CRT, green text and floppy | 8088, 64 KB, 5¼-inch floppy, CGA | boot media, text display, add-on video |
| 5–6 | **1995 / family PC** · lavender GUI desktop | Pentium 133, EDO, IDE, PCI, ISA audio | storage, buses, memory upgrade, multimedia |
| 7–8 | **2005 / blue LAN den** · flat panel and dark case | Socket 939 DDR, SATA, PCIe, Ethernet | compatibility, GPUs, networks, repairs |
| 9–10 | **2026 / creator lab** · high-contrast OLED-style display | AM5, DDR5, M.2 NVMe, PSU, cooling, GPU | power, thermals, integration, workloads |

Scenes change by era to create a sense of time travel. The UI uses one fixed navigation skeleton for learnability. These are representative systems, not exhaustive histories or literal reproductions of named brands.

## Session loops

- **60–120 seconds:** build and boot a small machine, learn a specific rule, collect 45–240 XP and 65–400 fictional credits.
- **5–10 minutes:** troubleshoot a more complicated job, buy a missing component, progress to the next era.
- **Optional next day:** a short question offers 30 credits + 25 XP; one claim per local calendar day and no lost streak.
- **Long term:** complete ten real configurations, collect three historic collector artifacts, share working blueprints. Roadmap later expands into circuit construction, soldering, DOS/BASIC, interfaces and computing generations.

## Ethical and platform rules

- Never require purchases to complete mandatory content.
- Do not introduce loot boxes, paid randomized rewards, deceptive scarcity or punitive engagement streaks to a child-oriented game.
- Any future account or trading must comply with child privacy and age verification/consent requirements in target jurisdictions, with robust moderation and parental oversight.
- No open chat or stranger friend requests in the first public release. Read-only blueprints can be shared privately.

## Product acceptance tests

- A first-time player can assemble and boot a working 1975 kit without reading a manual.
- A player with ordinary earned credits can complete all 10 levels; no hard currency softlock.
- A DDR2 RAM module in the Socket 939 DDR mission produces an actionable incompatibility explanation.
- All active-workbench parts cannot be lost via sale or NPC trade without a spare.
- Each reward can only be earned once per unique mission, local state survives refresh, and invalid link/saves do not execute arbitrary content.
- The phone layout has no 320–428px horizontal overflow, verified on a real browser before public beta.
- A learner can answer an unseen role/compatibility question better than before play in observed testing.

## Not in v0.2

Authoritative online player accounts and economies; real P2P trading; auctions; avatars; real-money monetization; AR/3D; exhaustive PC assembly; schematics, wiring and emulation; school reporting dashboards; analytics servers; teacher/admin features; friends/chat; cross-device cloud sync.
