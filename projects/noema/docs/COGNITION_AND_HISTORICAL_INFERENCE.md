# NOEMA Cognition and Historical Inference Protocol

## Purpose

NOEMA may connect modern biomedical and cognitive research to historical or ethnographic descriptions only as a **candidate explanatory layer**. Biomedical mechanism, cultural interpretation, historical role, and diagnosis are separate objects.

## Core rule

A resemblance between a historical description and a modern clinical or neurodevelopmental construct is not a diagnosis.

Examples of forbidden shortcuts:

- accused witch -> psychosis
- shaman -> autism or ADHD
- prophet -> epilepsy
- visionary -> hallucinatory disorder
- possession ritual -> dissociative disorder
- repetitive ritual -> autism

These may be research questions about phenomenology or mechanism, but they are not permissible entity labels without contemporaneous clinical evidence that meets the relevant diagnostic standard.

## Evidence channels

NOEMA separates five channels:

1. **Historical-cultural observation**: texts, court records, ethnography, archaeology, oral traditions, iconography.
2. **Phenomenology**: reported experience such as visions, trance, ecstasy, possession, dream states, felt presence, certainty or dissociation.
3. **Mechanism evidence**: modern neuroscience, psychiatry, psychology, pharmacology, sleep research or sensory science.
4. **Social-role evidence**: selection, training, status, stigma, institutional incentives and cultural scripts around specialists or accused persons.
5. **Clinical diagnosis**: requires appropriate contemporaneous clinical evidence and is normally unavailable for historical persons.

A relationship candidate must preserve which channel each observation belongs to.

## Candidate inference ladder

1. Similar wording or behavior is observed.
2. Phenomenological comparability is evaluated.
3. Source/translation bias is assessed.
4. Cultural-script and role-selection explanations are tested.
5. Environmental, trauma, substance, sleep and neurological alternatives are tested.
6. Modern mechanism literature may be linked as `POSSIBLE_MECHANISM_FOR`.
7. No diagnosis is emitted for historical persons or populations.
8. Stronger causal claims require independent evidence and explicit rival models.

## Required rival explanations

For every cognition/religion candidate relationship, test at least:

- cultural learning or expectation
- social-role selection
- institutional incentives
- ecology/material context
- trauma/stress
- sleep deprivation or dream phenomena
- substance/pharmacological effects
- neurological mechanism
- psychiatric mechanism
- neurodevelopmental variation
- translation/coding/source bias
- null/chance

Not every rival applies to every case, but omissions must be explained.

## Witchcraft-specific rule

`WITCH`, `WITCHCRAFT_ACCUSATION`, `MAGIC_PRACTITIONER`, and `RITUAL_SPECIALIST` must never be collapsed into one category.

European witch-trial records often describe accusations constructed through legal, theological, gendered, economic and local social processes. They are not clinical case files. Historical scholarship has specifically criticized the view that European witch hunts were primarily persecution of mentally ill people.

Research may instead ask narrower questions, for example:

- Were particular unusual experiences overrepresented in accusation narratives?
- Did culturally available demonological scripts shape how sleep paralysis, seizures, grief experiences or hallucinations were described?
- Were some healer/diviner roles socially compatible with traits that modern populations would describe as neurodivergent?
- Did institutions select or stigmatize people with unusual sensory, social, attentional or experiential profiles?

Any positive result remains a population-level historical hypothesis, not retrospective individual diagnosis.

## Neurodiversity-specific rule

NOEMA uses `NEURODEVELOPMENTAL_VARIATION` as a modern research domain, not as a historical identity category. Autism and ADHD are modern diagnostic constructs with changing definitions and ascertainment. Their current prevalence, genetics or behavioral correlates cannot be projected directly into historical populations.

Valid research questions include:

- whether contemporary neurodevelopmental traits correlate with particular forms of religiosity, ritual preference, sensory experience or social-role selection;
- whether historical roles contain documented trait descriptions that are phenomenologically comparable to modern traits;
- whether the same historical descriptions are better explained by training, cultural expectations, trauma, substances, sleep, neurological conditions or source bias.

## PubMed role

PubMed/MEDLINE is a **discovery and biomedical metadata source**. A PubMed record is not automatically evidence. The underlying paper must be reviewed for design, population, measures, limitations, conflicts and applicability before a claim is promoted.

## Publication language

Preferred labels:

- `phenomenological resemblance`
- `possible mechanism`
- `historical diagnostic proposal`
- `historical diagnostic proposal disputed`
- `cultural interpretation`
- `social-role association`

Avoid:

- `X was autistic`
- `X had ADHD`
- `witches were mentally ill`
- `mysticism is epilepsy`
- `shamanism is neurodivergence`

unless a modern, clinically observed population is explicitly being discussed and the source supports that exact claim.
