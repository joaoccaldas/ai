# NOEMA v0.9 — Human Meaning Atlas

## Purpose

The Human Meaning Atlas is NOEMA's first flagship space × time research surface. It turns reviewed-source projections into a visual deep-time lens without allowing the interface to manufacture geography, chronology, cultural continuity, theology, or species rankings.

Public route after activation:

- `projects/noema/site/human-meaning-atlas.html`
- generated projection: `projects/noema/site/human-meaning-atlas.json`

## Visual semantics

### Archaeological evidence pulses

A pulse exists only when a claim has:

1. a source record,
2. encoded temporal evidence,
3. a site with traceable coordinates.

Pulse color represents a broad evidence construct, not a religion or species score.

Pulse size uses evidence level only as a categorical visual encoding. It is not a posterior probability.

Halo dash style encodes the observation/inference band. A more interpretive halo is not visually promoted as stronger evidence.

### Time lens

The atlas domain runs from 500,000 BP to the present. The scrubber changes temporal emphasis. It does not claim that a behavior existed continuously between dated observations.

The oldest currently encoded observation is not rendered as an evolutionary origin point.

### D-PLACE coverage layer

The 100-society D-PLACE benchmark is a separate optional layer. It measures geographic and linguistic coverage only.

A D-PLACE point does **not** mean NOEMA has coded that society's religion, beliefs, ritual practices, mythology, or sacred traditions.

A D-PLACE focal year remains source observation/reference metadata, not a culture-origin date.

### Pattern signals

Descriptive signals may identify that a broad construct appears in more than one evidence pack. Such recurrence is a prompt for comparison only.

Cross-pack recurrence is not evidence, by itself, of:

- shared meaning,
- common ancestry,
- diffusion,
- inter-population transmission,
- independent convergence,
- a shared theology.

Those remain rival mechanisms requiring separate tests.

### Hypothesis field

Hypotheses without defensible geographic coordinates remain beside the globe. The UI must not invent map positions to make a theory visually complete.

## Forbidden inferences

The atlas generator and tests explicitly guard against:

- current oldest observation = evolutionary origin
- shared material form = shared meaning
- burial = afterlife doctrine
- ochre = ritual
- species ranking from evidence counts
- coverage point = coded belief evidence

## Failure behavior

If the deterministic atlas projection cannot load, the browser does not recreate the research analysis ad hoc. It shows an unavailable state instead.

If external world-outline geometry is unavailable, the evidence projection can still remain source-bounded; visual geographic context may degrade, but the research data must not be replaced with invented geometry.

## Next research expansion

The atlas becomes scientifically more valuable as new source adapters and reviewed dossiers add independently coded layers such as:

- mortuary practices
- ancestor relations
- divination
- altered-state practices
- shamanic roles
- sacred landscapes
- astronomical symbolism
- sacrifice
- supernatural agents
- afterlife concepts
- ritual synchrony
- personal ornament/display
- graphic and material symbolism

Each new layer must retain claim-level provenance, dating uncertainty, access restrictions, rivals/nulls, and source-dependence controls.
