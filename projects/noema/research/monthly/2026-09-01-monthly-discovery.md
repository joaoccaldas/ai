## Cross-domain findings

### 1. Mortuary membership ↔ biological kinship
**Strength: high candidate.**

Independent ancient-DNA studies now provide a useful comparison across different northern-European contexts. At Ajvide/Gotland, co-buried Pitted Ware individuals were close genetic relatives, including beyond first degree. In Neolithic Caithness/Orkney tombs, many males in the same or nearby tombs were related through paternal lines, while the authors explicitly frame kinship as broader than biology.

This supports a narrow candidate: **biological relatedness can be a major variable in burial membership and funerary architecture**. It does *not* support “ancestor worship” as the default explanation.

Rivals: (1) socially salient descent/kin ideology; (2) household/local cemetery access producing kin clustering without an explicit descent ideology; (3) broader social kin/affiliation only partly correlated with biology.

Null: the relatedness excess disappears after local population structure, cemetery sampling and household spatial autocorrelation are modeled.

Best discriminator: multi-site genomes plus contemporaneous background controls, isotope mobility/residence data, and explicit models separating kin selection from spatial proximity.

### 2. Sensory ritual technology ↔ altered-experience affordances
**Strength: medium-high candidate.**

The new Etruscan tomb study measures substantial acoustic differences and models divergent sensory trajectories. Its acoustic measurements are observations; its trance-like/cognitive-state reconstruction is an interpretation. Independent neuroscience and psychophysiology show that rhythmic auditory stimulation can alter experience, and newer immersive-context experiments support treating context/medium separately from phenomenology.

Candidate: **ritual environments may repeatedly exploit controllable sensory intensity, rhythm, enclosure, darkness or immersion to shape attention/arousal/absorption**.

Rivals: (1) intentional sensory design; (2) accidental architectural effects later incorporated into ritual; (3) modern category projection onto ancient spaces.

Null: ritual structures do not differ from matched non-ritual controls, or their sensory properties have no reproducible perceptual effect once expectations are controlled.

Best discriminator: preregistered acoustic/control samples and blind reconstructions that independently manipulate geometry, sound, lighting, imagery and expectation.

### 3. Collective synchrony/arousal ↔ cohesion/cooperation
**Strength: medium-high candidate.**

A field experiment manipulating synchrony and arousal found effects on clustering/cooperation. A separate naturalistic ancestor festival study found associations between active participation, ancestral belief and several cohesion/prosocial outcomes.

Candidate: **synchrony plus arousal is a plausible recurrent mechanism for some short-run social effects of collective ritual**. It is not a universal function of ritual.

Rivals: pre-existing commitment/selection into participation; common goals/shared attention rather than synchrony; local institutional incentives.

Null: synchrony adds no effect after shared goals, prior commitment, demand characteristics and arousal are controlled.

Best discriminator: preregistered multi-cultural factorial studies and longitudinal designs separating selection from post-participation change.

### 4. Sacred-place norms ↔ ecological protection
**Strength: medium candidate.**

Sacred natural sites recur widely and some controlled studies report greater biodiversity/habitat diversity than surrounding land.

Candidate: **customary sacred restrictions can function as de facto conservation institutions**.

The causal arrow is unresolved. Biodiverse/remnant locations may become sacred, and remoteness/topography/governance can cause both sacred-site persistence and ecological protection.

Null: sacred status has no independent effect after habitat, access, land-use history and governance are matched.

Best discriminator: matched sacred/non-sacred sites plus historical remote-sensing trajectories and independent ethnography of actual extraction rules.

### 5. Astronomical orientation ↔ sacred architecture
**Strength: fragile candidate.**

Astronomical orientation plainly occurs in some monument traditions, but the literature also contains claimed alignments that fail when researchers use predefined selection criteria and pseudo-random simulations.

Candidate retained only at the population/distribution level: **some architectural traditions intentionally encode celestial orientation**. Individual “hits” are weak evidence without a complete sampling frame.

Rivals: topography/access/cardinal planning; post-hoc celestial target choice; within-tradition architectural ancestry.

Null: alignments are no better than the available orientation/horizon and researcher-selection space.

Best discriminator: complete monument samples, preregistered targets/tolerances, topographic controls and Monte Carlo nulls.

### 6. Cross-hominin deliberate material salience
**Strength: medium-high candidate.**

Recent work adds to a growing record of deliberate marks, pigment use and non-utilitarian/material-display practices associated with Neanderthals, while 2026 Aurignacian traceology demonstrates why pigment residues must be separated from assumptions of intentional symbolic coloration. A 2026 conceptual analysis argues that “behavioral modernity” is no longer a clean species-distinguishing umbrella.

Candidate: **compare operational components across Homo populations instead of treating “symbolic behavior” or “behavioral modernity” as a single latent trait**.

Rivals: (1) independent convergence under similar cognitive/social constraints; (2) deeper shared biological capacity without transmission of a particular cultural convention; (3) category inflation combining utilitarian, aesthetic, display and semiotic practices.

Null: once semantic labels are removed, no coherent cross-hominin construct remains beyond generic material manipulation.

Best discriminator: blind component-level coding, direct traceology/experimental work, secure chronology/population attribution, and explicit contact/admixture models where populations overlap.

## High-independence recurrences worth keeping visible

Two recurrences deserve continued attention without metaphysical interpretation:

- **Rhythmic sound and nonordinary experience:** culturally separated practices repeatedly use rhythmic stimulation; laboratory/neurobiological findings make a shared physiological convergence mechanism plausible. What remains unexplained is why specific rhythms, settings and ritual packages are culturally selected and stabilized.
- **Kin-structured burial:** close relatedness affects burial grouping in distinct social/subsistence systems, but the social interpretation can range from genealogy and corporate identity to local cemetery access.

These are unexplained or partially explained empirical patterns, not supernatural findings.
