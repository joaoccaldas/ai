# NOEMA v1.7 — Biobehavioral Evidence & Historical Interpretation

## Goal

Extend NOEMA beyond comparative religion/archaeology into a carefully governed biomedical and behavioral evidence layer that can test cross-domain hypotheses about altered states, cognition, neurodevelopmental traits, social perception, dissociation, epilepsy, psychedelics, sleep/dreaming, psychosis-spectrum phenomena, spirituality, ritual and historical interpretation.

## Scientific boundary

NOEMA must never retro-diagnose historical individuals or populations from sparse narrative records. Modern biomedical constructs are not historical identities.

The admissible inference ladder is:

1. **Modern observed association** — a peer-reviewed biomedical study reports an association between a measured trait/state and a religious/spiritual/altered-state outcome.
2. **Phenomenological overlap** — a historically described experience resembles one or more modern measured phenomena.
3. **Interpretive hypothesis** — the overlap may help generate a hypothesis about how an experience could have been culturally interpreted.
4. **Historical mechanism test** — independent historical, legal, demographic, ecological and cultural evidence must support any claim about persecution, institutional response, diffusion or social role.

Steps 2–4 must never be collapsed into a diagnosis.

## Example: witchcraft / persecution

Allowed questions:

- Do historical descriptions of visions, seizures, trance, unusual sensory experiences, sleep paralysis, possession or ecstatic behavior overlap with phenomena studied in modern neurology/psychiatry?
- Were such descriptions interpreted differently across legal/religious regimes?
- Did gender, status, local conflict, famine, epidemic, doctrinal change or judicial procedure better predict accusation than any phenomenological category?
- Are there regions/periods where medicalized descriptions and witchcraft/possession labels co-occur in the historical record?

Disallowed shortcuts:

- "witches were autistic/ADHD/epileptic/schizophrenic"
- diagnosis of named historical people without contemporary clinical evidence
- using a modern trait prevalence study as proof of a historical causal mechanism
- treating psychiatric/neurological explanation and religious meaning as mutually exclusive categories

## New source family: PubMed / NLM

### Role

Tier A/B hybrid depending on record type:

- peer-reviewed biomedical literature discovery
- MeSH-normalized concepts
- PMID/PMCID/DOI identity
- publication type and journal metadata
- clinical/experimental/review evidence on cognition, neurodevelopment, altered states, sleep, epilepsy, psychedelics, dissociation, psychosis, spirituality and religion

### Evidence policy

- PubMed indexing metadata is discovery/context, not itself evidence.
- Scientific claims come from the indexed paper, not the PMID record.
- Reviews and primary studies remain distinct evidence types.
- Case reports never support population-level prevalence or historical causation.
- Modern diagnostic constructs cannot be back-projected as historical diagnoses.
- Cultural framing/set/setting is an explicit confounder for religious and psychedelic experience.

## Cross-domain candidate relationships

Examples to test, never assume:

1. `ALTERED_STATE_INDUCTION ↔ MYSTICAL_TYPE_EXPERIENCE`
2. `SEIZURE_EPILEPSY ↔ RELIGIOUS_EXPERIENCE`
3. `DISSOCIATION ↔ POSSESSION_INTERPRETATION`
4. `SLEEP_DREAMING ↔ SUPERNATURAL_AGENT_PERCEPTION`
5. `AUTISTIC_TRAITS ↔ RELIGIOSITY/SPIRITUALITY` (direction may differ by construct and sample)
6. `SCHIZOTYPAL_TRAITS ↔ LIFE_CHANGING_RSE`
7. `SET_SETTING ↔ PSYCHEDELIC_MYSTICAL_INTERPRETATION`
8. historical `VISION/TRANCE/SEIZURE` descriptions ↔ historical `POSSESSION/WITCHCRAFT/PROPHECY` labels, with legal/cultural regime as mandatory moderator

## Required confounders for historical interpretation

- sex/gender
- age
- socioeconomic status
- kinship/local disputes
- religious denomination/doctrine
- legal procedure and evidentiary standards
- war/famine/epidemic/environmental stress
- literacy and record survival
- colonial/missionary framing
- source genre
- retrospective transcription
- judicial torture/coercion
- language/translation
- publication/archival selection

## Validation plan

1. Add PubMed adapter producing candidate-only records with PMID/PMCID/DOI/MeSH/publication-type provenance.
2. Add source-specific tests for dedupe, publication-type preservation, case-report guardrails and MeSH normalization.
3. Add explicit `NO_RETRO_DIAGNOSIS` guard to public analytical projections.
4. Add a cross-domain hypothesis template requiring modern evidence + historical evidence + moderator/confounder set.
5. Add UI lens `Biobehavioral` under Research, not as a default explanatory overlay.
6. Build a pilot corpus around:
   - autism/schizotypy and religiosity/spirituality
   - temporal-lobe epilepsy and religious experience
   - trance/possession/dissociation
   - psychedelics and mystical-type experience
   - sleep/dreaming and supernatural-agent perception
7. Only after the pilot, test historical witchcraft/possession corpora using non-diagnostic phenomenology categories.

## Success criteria

- No historical diagnosis is emitted by code or UI.
- Every cross-domain relationship names its source family and evidence type.
- Case reports cannot become population-level pattern candidates.
- PubMed metadata never bypasses claim review.
- Historical analyses display competing social/legal explanations alongside biobehavioral hypotheses.
- Any apparent correlation is explicitly tested against source dependence, geography, time, record intensity and missingness.
