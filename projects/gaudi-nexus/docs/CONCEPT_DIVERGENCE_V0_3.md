# CONCEPT_DIVERGENCE_V0.3 — 2026-10-04

## Purpose

Generate five **materially different** architectural systems on the real plaza geometry, normalize them to the same ~1,800 m² GFA assumption, preserve a first-pass monument-view proxy, and refuse to select a winner from image appeal.

These are abstract massing hypotheses, not architecture yet.

## Common assumptions

- plaza geometry: SITE_TRUTH_V0.2
- target GFA: ~1,800 m²
- protected-view proxy: 24 m wide, geometry-derived, not yet authoritative
- no photorealism
- no fake structural claims
- no claim that the internal screen equals the competition jury rubric

## Five hypotheses

| ID | concept | ground footprint | plaza occupation | levels / below grade | view-proxy overlap |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| A | Living Threshold | 900 m² | 7.74% | 2 levels | 0 m² |
| B | Inverted Plaza | 450 m² above | 3.87% | 75% program below grade | 0 m² |

| D | Urban Forest | 900 m² | 7.74% | 2 levels / 9 components | 0 m² |

## Internal screening result

A deliberately narrow heuristic tested only:
- public-ground retention,
- view-proxy protection,
- fragmentation,
- below-grade dependency.

It produced:

1. **Cerdà Mutation — 8.90/10**
2. **Inverted Plaza — 8.30/10**
3. **Living Threshold — 8.22/10**
4. **Market Organism — 7.72/10**
5. **Urban Forest — 6.72/10**

This ranking is **not architectural judgment** and is **not the official 100-point jury score**.

## Why C currently leads

C combines:
- very low ground occupation;
- zero overlap with the current view proxy;
- direct dialogue with the Eixample grid;
- the possibility of translating Gaudí's ruled/structural logic inside Cerdà rather than cosmetically imitating Gaudí.

### Fatal risks
- three levels may be too vertically assertive next to the monument;
- bars may feel formalistic rather than civic;
- market logistics may become linear and inefficient;
- sections must prove daylight, public permeability and humane scale.

## Why B is dangerous-but-interesting

The Inverted Plaza preserves public ground best.

### Fatal risks
- below-grade market/community program can become bunker-like;
- excavation, waterproofing and cost can overwhelm the concept;
- natural light, orientation and emergency egress are difficult;
- it may solve the visual problem while damaging everyday civic experience.

## Why A remains alive

Living Threshold is strongest conceptually because it directly addresses the resident/visitor conflict.

### Fatal risks
- split massing can duplicate circulation/service infrastructure;
- two edges can become barriers rather than thresholds;
- the idea must be proved in section and behavior, not copywriting.

## Why D currently trails

Urban Forest is porous and emotionally attractive, but nine components create:
- service duplication;
- structural repetition;
- weather-protection complexity;
- acoustic leakage;
- difficult operational control.

It survives only if the distributed system produces a social/environmental advantage impossible with fewer elements.

## Why E remains strategically useful

Market Organism could create the best operational market.

### Fatal risks
- "spine" can become a corridor rather than civic space;
- too many modules can read as object collection;
- its Gaudí relationship is currently weak.

## Next gate: architecture, not massing

Every scheme must now produce the same proof package:

1. program schedule by room/use;
2. 1:500 plan;
3. longitudinal and transverse section;
4. service/loading path;
5. accessible route;
6. structural diagram;
7. summer comfort / shade logic;
8. one human-scale perspective;
9. explicit Gaudí-method translation;
10. fatal-flaw list.

Only then may two finalists be selected.
