# Studio 2 Art Direction

## Creative thesis

**Cozy weird cinematic software archaeology.**

The world should feel mysterious, funny, warm and technically credible at the same time. It must never become a franchise imitation. No recognizable characters, marks, signature props or copied scenes.

### Emotional mix

- mystery: 35%
- humor: 25%
- wonder: 20%
- technical mastery: 15%
- melancholy: 5%

## Narrative visual progression

### 01 · The Cave
Primitive geometry, firelight, crude glowing diagrams, low-poly fossils of old UI. Deliberately rough and tactile.

### 02 · The Workshop
Warm wood, exposed cables, tools, mismatched terminals, sparks and half-built machines. Chaos that feels human rather than dangerous.

### 03 · The Cemetery
Moonlight, fog, stone terminals, CRT headstones, roots through obsolete machines. Deadpan labels. One empty grave should imply a resurrected project.

### 04 · The Arcade
Retro pixel language at the threshold, deeper modern rendering at close range. Cabinets can represent playable, prototype, archived and source-only states.

### 05 · The Observatory
The joke density drops. Dark dome, precise orbital graphics, high-quality materials and large negative space. This act should create genuine awe.

### 06 · The Machine
The most precise area. Glass, brushed metal, flowing data, agent nodes and clean interaction. The visual payoff for everything that came before it.

### 07 · The Horizon
Unfinished white/wireframe architecture, scaffolds, floating components and construction lights. Future projects are not rendered as finished products.

## Humor rules

Humor should be dry and specific, never clownish. Project postmortems may use lines such as “worked once” or “scope exceeded available adulthood,” but the technical explanation beneath it stays rigorous.

## Materials

Use a restrained reusable kit: rough basalt, aged wood, painted metal, glass, emissive CRT, stone, carbon, brushed aluminum and wireframe construction material. Avoid a separate bespoke shader system per room.

## Lighting

Most depth should come from baked or static lighting for authored Blender scenes. Runtime lights are for focal accents and interactive state. Use fog and emissive surfaces instead of many shadow-casting lights.
